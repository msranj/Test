

# Grade 4 - English Grammar Notes

## Clear explanations, everyday examples and friendly practice

**For students:** Read one topic at a time. Notice the examples, try the

## 1. Compound Nouns

### What is a compound noun?

A **compound noun** is a naming word made by joining two or more words.
The new word names one person, place, animal, thing or idea.

Think of it like making a new toy by joining two building blocks. The
two words come together to make one name.

### Everyday examples

-   **tooth + brush = toothbrush** -- something we use to clean our
    teeth.
-   **rain + coat = raincoat** -- something we wear in the rain.
-   **class + room = classroom** -- the room where we learn.
-   **sun + flower = sunflower** -- a flower that looks towards the sun.

### Three ways compound nouns can be written

1.  **One word:** football, bedroom, toothpaste
2.  **Two separate words:** bus stop, dining table, swimming pool
3.  **Words joined with a hyphen (-):** mother-in-law, check-in

### Remember

The words work together to name one thing. For example, a *toothbrush*
is one object, not a tooth and a brush being discussed separately.

### Try it

1.  Join the words: class + mate = \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
2.  What compound noun names a coat worn in the rain?
    \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
3.  Find the compound noun: "We waited at the bus stop."
4.  Make a compound noun using **bed**.
5.  Write one compound noun you see or use at home.

**Answers:** 1. classmate 2. raincoat 3. bus stop 4. bedroom/bedtime
(either suitable answer) 5. Answers will vary.

## 2. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

### Countable nouns

A **countable noun** names something we can count one by one. It has a
singular form and a plural form.

Examples: one pencil, two pencils; one mango, five mangoes; one chair,
four chairs.

We can use **a/an** with one countable item: - a book - an orange

We can use numbers with countable nouns: - three notebooks - ten
students

### Uncountable nouns

An **uncountable noun** names something we usually measure, rather than
count one by one.

Examples: water, milk, rice, sugar, sand, air.

We do not usually say *two waters* or *three rices* when we mean the
substance. We can say: - a glass of water - two bottles of milk - a bowl
of rice - three spoonfuls of sugar

### A simple kitchen example

Imagine making breakfast. You can count **eggs** (one egg, two eggs),
but you usually measure **milk** in a glass or cup.

### Words to use

-   Countable: **many**, a few, several
-   Uncountable: **much**, a little, some
-   Both: **some** and **a lot of** can be used with both, depending on
    the sentence.

Examples: - How **many** apples are there? - How **much** water is in
the bottle? - We have **some** bananas. - There is **some** rice.

### Remember

Some nouns can be used in different ways depending on what we mean. For
example, *chicken* can mean the animal (countable: three chickens) or
the meat (uncountable: some chicken).

### Try it

Write **C** for countable and **U** for uncountable. 1. notebook 2.
honey 3. chairs 4. flour 5. grapes

Choose the correct word: 6. How (many/much) juice is left? 7. How
(many/much) students are in your class?

**Answers:** 1. C 2. U 3. C 4. U 5. C 6. much 7. many
