**For students:** Read one topic at a time. Notice the examples, try the

## 1. Compound Nouns

### What is a compound noun?

A **compound noun** is a naming word made by joining two or more words.
The new word names one person, place, animal, thing or idea.

Think of it like making a new toy by joining two building blocks. The
two words come together to make one name.

### Everyday examples

-   **tooth + brush = toothbrush** -- something we use to clean our
    teeth.
-   **rain + coat = raincoat** -- something we wear in the rain.
-   **class + room = classroom** -- the room where we learn.
-   **sun + flower = sunflower** -- a flower that looks towards the sun.

### Three ways compound nouns can be written

1.  **One word:** football, bedroom, toothpaste
2.  **Two separate words:** bus stop, dining table, swimming pool
3.  **Words joined with a hyphen (-):** mother-in-law, check-in

### Remember

The words work together to name one thing. For example, a *toothbrush*
is one object, not a tooth and a brush being discussed separately.

### Try it

1.  Join the words: class + mate = \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
2.  What compound noun names a coat worn in the rain?
    \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
3.  Find the compound noun: "We waited at the bus stop."
4.  Make a compound noun using **bed**.
5.  Write one compound noun you see or use at home.

**Answers:** 1. classmate 2. raincoat 3. bus stop 4. bedroom/bedtime
(either suitable answer) 5. Answers will vary.

## 2. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

### Countable nouns

A **countable noun** names something we can count one by one. It has a
singular form and a plural form.

Examples: one pencil, two pencils; one mango, five mangoes; one chair,
four chairs.

We can use **a/an** with one countable item: - a book - an orange

We can use numbers with countable nouns: - three notebooks - ten
students

### Uncountable nouns

An **uncountable noun** names something we usually measure, rather than
count one by one.

Examples: water, milk, rice, sugar, sand, air.

We do not usually say *two waters* or *three rices* when we mean the
substance. We can say: - a glass of water - two bottles of milk - a bowl
of rice - three spoonfuls of sugar

### A simple kitchen example

Imagine making breakfast. You can count **eggs** (one egg, two eggs),
but you usually measure **milk** in a glass or cup.

### Words to use

-   Countable: **many**, a few, several
-   Uncountable: **much**, a little, some
-   Both: **some** and **a lot of** can be used with both, depending on
    the sentence.

Examples: - How **many** apples are there? - How **much** water is in
the bottle? - We have **some** bananas. - There is **some** rice.

### Remember

Some nouns can be used in different ways depending on what we mean. For
example, *chicken* can mean the animal (countable: three chickens) or
the meat (uncountable: some chicken).

### Try it

Write **C** for countable and **U** for uncountable. 1. notebook 2.
honey 3. chairs 4. flour 5. grapes

Choose the correct word: 6. How (many/much) juice is left? 7. How
(many/much) students are in your class?

**Answers:** 1. C 2. U 3. C 4. U 5. C 6. much 7. many

## 3. General Pronouns

### What is a pronoun?

A **pronoun** is a word we use instead of a naming word (noun). It helps
us avoid repeating the same name again and again.

Example: - Without a pronoun: "Asha has a bag. Asha keeps Asha's books
in the bag." - With pronouns: "Asha has a bag. **She** keeps **her**
books in it."

### Useful kinds of pronouns

**1. Personal pronouns** -- name the person or people speaking,
listening or being spoken about. - I, me, we, us, you, he, him, she,
her, it, they, them

**2. Possessive pronouns** -- show that something belongs to someone. -
mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs

**3. Demonstrative pronouns** -- point to people or things. - this,
that, these, those - "**This** is my pencil." "**Those** are your
shoes."

**4. Interrogative pronouns** -- help us ask questions. - who, whom,
whose, which, what - "**Who** is at the door?" "**Which** is your
bottle?"

**5. Reflexive pronouns** -- refer back to the person doing the
action. - myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves,
yourselves, themselves - "I packed my school bag **myself**."

**6. Indefinite pronouns** -- refer to people or things without saying
exactly who or what. - someone, anyone, everyone, nobody, something,
anything, everything - "**Someone** left a lunch box here."

### Remember

A pronoun takes the place of a noun. Check that your reader can
understand who or what the pronoun refers to.

### Try it

1.  Replace "Rohan" with a pronoun: "Rohan is reading."
2.  Which word is a pronoun? "The cat is sleeping. It is tired."
3.  Choose a pronoun: "\_\_\_\_\_ is knocking at the door." (Who /
    Table)
4.  Find the pronoun: "These are my crayons."
5.  Complete: "I made this card by \_\_\_\_\_."

**Answers:** 1. He 2. It 3. Who 4. These 5. myself

## 4. Personal Pronouns

### What are personal pronouns?

Personal pronouns stand for the person or people speaking, the person or
people listening, or the person, animal or thing being spoken about.

Imagine three groups in a classroom conversation:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Group                   Pronouns                Example
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  The speaker             I, me, we, us           **I** am ready. **We**
                                                  are ready.

  The listener            you                     **You** are my friend.

  The person or thing     he, him, she, her, it,  **She** is kind.
  spoken about            they, them              **They** are playing.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Subject and object forms

A subject pronoun usually tells us who does the action: - **I** kicked
the ball. - **They** planted a tree.

An object pronoun usually tells us who receives the action: - Dad called
**me**. - I helped **him**. - The teacher praised **us**.

### Singular and plural

-   Usually one: I, me, you, he, him, she, her, it
-   More than one: we, us, you, they, them

**You** can mean one person or more than one person.

### Common mistakes

-   Correct: "Me and Riya went to school" is commonly said in
    conversation, but in careful school writing use **"Riya and I went
    to school."**
-   Correct: "The teacher gave **him** a book," not "The teacher gave he
    a book."

### Try it

Choose the correct pronoun. 1. (He/Him) is my brother. 2. Mother called
(I/me). 3. Riya and (I/me) are friends. 4. The coach praised
(they/them). 5. The dog wagged (it/its) tail. *(Choose the personal
pronoun that completes the sentence.)*

**Answers:** 1. He 2. me 3. I 4. them 5. it

## 5. Possessive Adjectives and Possessive Pronouns

Both types show **ownership** -- who something belongs to.

### Possessive adjectives

A possessive adjective comes **before a naming word**. It tells us whose
thing it is.

-   my pencil
-   your bottle
-   his cap
-   her bag
-   its tail
-   our classroom
-   their house

Example: "This is **my** lunch box." The word *my* comes before *lunch
box*.

### Possessive pronouns

A possessive pronoun replaces the naming word and can stand on its own.

-   mine
-   yours
-   his
-   hers
-   ours
-   theirs

Example: - "This lunch box is **mine**." - "That blue bag is **hers**."

### Compare them

  Possessive adjective      Possessive pronoun
  ------------------------- --------------------------
  This is **my** book.      This book is **mine**.
  Is that **your** ruler?   Is that ruler **yours**?
  It is **their** house.    The house is **theirs**.

### Important

-   **Its** shows that something belongs to an animal or thing: "The
    bird cleaned **its** feathers."
-   **It's** means **it is** or **it has**: "**It's** raining." The
    apostrophe changes the meaning.

### Try it

1.  This is (my/mine) pencil.
2.  The red bicycle is (our/ours).
3.  The puppy is wagging (its/it's) tail.
4.  That water bottle belongs to Sara. It is (her/hers).
5.  This classroom belongs to us. It is (our/ours).

**Answers:** 1. my 2. ours 3. its 4. hers 5. ours

## 6. The -ing Form

### What is the -ing form?

We often add **-ing** to a verb (an action word) to show an action that
is happening or to use the action as the name of an activity.

Examples: - play → playing - read → reading - jump → jumping - draw →
drawing

### How to make the -ing form

1.  **Usually add -ing:** walk → walking; help → helping.
2.  **If a word ends in silent e, usually remove the e and add -ing:**
    make → making; dance → dancing.
3.  **For some short words ending in one vowel and one consonant, double
    the last consonant:** run → running; sit → sitting; hop → hopping.
4.  **If a word ends in -ie, change -ie to y and add -ing:** lie →
    lying; tie → tying.

### When do we use it?

**1. An action happening now** - I am **reading** a story. - The
children are **playing** outside. - Dad is **cooking** dinner.

We often use **am/is/are + verb-ing**.

**2. An activity as a thing** - **Swimming** is fun. - I enjoy
**drawing**. - **Reading** helps us learn.

### Remember

Look at the whole sentence. In "She is painting," painting tells us what
she is doing now. In "Painting is my hobby," painting names an activity.

### Try it

Write the -ing form. 1. write 2. swim 3. clean 4. dance 5. lie

Complete the sentences: 6. The baby is \_\_\_\_\_ (sleep). 7. \_\_\_\_\_
(read) is my favourite activity.

**Answers:** 1. writing 2. swimming 3. cleaning 4. dancing 5. lying 6.
sleeping 7. Reading

## 7. Past Participles and Perfect Tenses

### What is a past participle?

A **past participle** is a special form of a verb. We use it with **has,
have** or **had** to talk about actions that are completed.

Examples: - play → played - clean → cleaned - eat → eaten - write →
written - see → seen - go → gone

Some verbs form the past participle by adding **-ed**. Others change in
a different way, so we learn them as we read and write.

### Three perfect tenses

**1. Present perfect: has/have + past participle**

Use it for an action that has happened and is connected to now. - I
**have finished** my homework. - She **has eaten** breakfast. - They
**have visited** the zoo.

Use **has** with he, she, it and one person or thing. Use **have** with
I, you, we, they and plural naming words.

**2. Past perfect: had + past participle**

Use it to show that one action was completed before another past
action. - I **had packed** my bag before the bus arrived. - The film
**had started** before we entered.

**3. Future perfect: will have + past participle**

Use it for an action that will be completed before a time in the
future. - By 8 o'clock, I **will have finished** dinner. - By Friday, we
**will have completed** the project.

### A simple timeline

-   Present perfect: completed action connected to now.
-   Past perfect: completed before another past event.
-   Future perfect: completed before a future time.

### Common verb forms

  Base verb   Past form   Past participle
  ----------- ----------- -----------------
  play        played      played
  go          went        gone
  eat         ate         eaten
  write       wrote       written
  see         saw         seen
  break       broke       broken
  take        took        taken
  do          did         done

### Try it

1.  The children have \_\_\_\_\_ (finish) their work.
2.  Mother has \_\_\_\_\_ (make) a cake.
3.  We had \_\_\_\_\_ (leave) before it rained.
4.  By tomorrow, I will have \_\_\_\_\_ (complete) the chart.
5.  Write the past participle of **see**.

**Answers:** 1. finished 2. made 3. left 4. completed 5. seen

## 8. Have

### What does "have" mean?

We use **have** and **has** to talk about owning or having something,
and to talk about experiences or activities.

Examples: - I **have** a new school bag. - She **has** a blue water
bottle. - We **have** lunch at school.

### Have or has?

  Use **have** with   Use **has** with
  ------------------- -------------------------------------
  I                   he
  you                 she
  we                  it
  they                one person or thing: Rohan, the dog

Examples: - I **have** two pencils. - You **have** a lovely smile. - The
children **have** new books. - My sister **has** a doll. - The school
**has** a playground.

### Using "had"

**Had** is used with all subjects to talk about having something in the
past. - I **had** a red bicycle. - She **had** a fever yesterday. - They
**had** a picnic last Sunday.

### Questions and negative sentences

-   **Do** you have a pencil?
-   **Does** he have a pencil?
-   I **do not (don't)** have a pencil.
-   She **does not (doesn't)** have a pencil.
-   **Did** they have a holiday yesterday?

Notice: after **does** or **did**, use **have**, not *has* or *had*. -
Correct: Does she **have** a pet? - Correct: Did he **have** breakfast?

### Have/has as helping words

We also use have/has with a past participle: - I **have finished**. - He
**has gone** home.

### Try it

1.  My brother (have/has) a cricket bat.
2.  We (have/has) English today.
3.  She (have/had) a cold yesterday.
4.  Does Riya (has/have) a sister?
5.  They have \_\_\_\_\_ (eat) their lunch.

**Answers:** 1. has 2. have 3. had 4. have 5. eaten

## 9. Agreement of the Verb with the Subject

### What does it mean?

A sentence has a **subject** (who or what we are talking about) and a
**verb** (the action or state). The verb must match the subject.

Think of it like matching a pair of shoes: the two parts must go
together.

### The basic rule

-   With **he, she, it** or one person/thing, we usually add **-s** or
    **-es** to the verb in the simple present.
-   With **I, you, we, they** or more than one person/thing, we use the
    base form of the verb.

  Subject    Correct sentence
  ---------- --------------------
  I          I play football.
  You        You play football.
  He         He plays football.
  She        She watches TV.
  The dog    The dog barks.
  We         We play football.
  The dogs   The dogs bark.

### Special examples

-   I **am** happy.
-   He **is** happy.
-   They **are** happy.
-   She **has** a kite.
-   We **have** kites.
-   The child **does** the work.
-   The children **do** the work.

### Watch out

A subject can have extra words after it. Find the main person or
thing: - "The **box** of crayons **is** on the table." The subject is
*box*, not *crayons*. - "My **friends** are coming." The subject is
*friends*.

### Try it

Choose the correct verb. 1. The boy (run/runs) fast. 2. My friends
(is/are) helpful. 3. The cat (sleep/sleeps) on the sofa. 4. I (has/have)
a new notebook. 5. The basket of apples (is/are) heavy.

**Answers:** 1. runs 2. are 3. sleeps 4. have 5. is

## 12. Adjectives I: Qualitative, Quantitative and Demonstrative

### What is an adjective?

An **adjective** is a describing word. It gives us more information
about a naming word.

Imagine describing your school bag: - a **blue** bag - a **heavy** bag -
**two** bags - **this** bag

The words in bold help us understand which bag or what kind of bag.

### 1. Qualitative adjectives -- what kind?

These adjectives tell us about a quality or feature. - a **kind**
teacher - a **sweet** mango - a **round** ball - a **bright** classroom

Ask: **What kind?**

### 2. Quantitative adjectives -- how much or how many?

These tell us about an amount or number. - **five** pencils - **many**
students - **some** water - **little** sugar

Ask: **How much?** or **How many?**

### 3. Demonstrative adjectives -- which one?

These point out a particular naming word. - **this** book (near, one) -
**that** tree (far, one) - **these** pencils (near, more than one) -
**those** birds (far, more than one)

Ask: **Which one or which ones?**

### Remember the four pointing words

  Word    Use it for                         Example
  ------- ---------------------------------- ---------------
  this    one thing near                     this pencil
  that    one thing farther away             that building
  these   more than one thing near           these flowers
  those   more than one thing farther away   those stars

### Try it

Name the type of adjective in bold. 1. We saw a **beautiful** butterfly.
2. There are **seven** days in a week. 3. **Those** shoes are new. 4.
Please give me **some** rice. 5. It was a **cold** morning.

**Answers:** 1. Qualitative 2. Quantitative 3. Demonstrative 4.
Quantitative 5. Qualitative

## 13. Adjectives II: Degrees of Comparison

### Why do we compare?

We compare things when we want to say that one is tall, taller or the
tallest. Adjectives can show three degrees of comparison.

Imagine three towers made from building blocks: - Tower A is **tall**. -
Tower B is **taller** than Tower A. - Tower C is the **tallest** of all
three.

### The three degrees

1.  **Positive:** describes one person or thing -- tall.
2.  **Comparative:** compares two -- taller.
3.  **Superlative:** compares three or more -- tallest.

### How to form them

  Positive    Comparative      Superlative
  ----------- ---------------- ----------------
  small       smaller          smallest
  tall        taller           tallest
  nice        nicer            nicest
  happy       happier          happiest
  big         bigger           biggest
  beautiful   more beautiful   most beautiful
  good        better           best
  bad         worse            worst

### Useful rules

-   Many short adjectives take **-er** and **-est**: fast, faster,
    fastest.
-   If an adjective ends in **y**, it often changes to **i**: happy,
    happier, happiest.
-   Some short words double the last letter: big, bigger, biggest.
-   Longer adjectives often use **more** and **most**: careful, more
    careful, most careful.
-   Some words change completely: good, better, best.

### Use them correctly

-   "My bag is **heavier than** yours." (comparing two)
-   "This is the **heaviest** bag in the class." (comparing more than
    two)
-   "A mango is **sweet**." (describing one)

Avoid using two comparison forms together: - Correct: more beautiful -
Incorrect: more beautifuler

### Try it

1.  Write the comparative form of **small**.
2.  Write the superlative form of **happy**.
3.  Choose: This road is (longer/longest) than that road.
4.  Choose: Mount Everest is the (higher/highest) mountain in the world.
5.  Complete: good, better, \_\_\_\_\_.

**Answers:** 1. smaller 2. happiest 3. longer 4. highest 5. best

## 14. Prepositions

### What is a preposition?

A **preposition** is a small word that shows how one thing is connected
to another. It often tells us where something is, when something happens
or which direction something moves.

### 1. Prepositions of place -- where?

Imagine a toy car on a table: - The car is **on** the table. - The car
is **under** the table. - The car is **beside** the table. - The car is
**behind** the table. - The car is **between** two books. - The car is
**in** the box.

### 2. Prepositions of time -- when?

-   **at** 7 o'clock
-   **on** Monday
-   **in** June
-   **in** the morning
-   **before** lunch
-   **after** school

A helpful guide: - **at** -- an exact time: at 6:30 - **on** -- a day or
date: on Friday, on 12 June - **in** -- a month, year or part of the
day: in July, in 2026, in the evening

### 3. Prepositions of direction or movement

-   Walk **to** the gate.
-   The bird flew **into** the room.
-   The bus went **through** the tunnel.
-   We walked **across** the road.
-   The ball rolled **towards** the door.

### Everyday example

When you say, "I put my lunch box **inside** my school bag **before**
leaving **for** school," the prepositions explain where and when things
happen.

### Try it

Choose a suitable preposition. 1. The picture is \_\_\_\_\_ the wall.
(on/in) 2. We go to school \_\_\_\_\_ Monday. (on/at) 3. The keys are
\_\_\_\_\_ the drawer. (in/at) 4. The train passed \_\_\_\_\_ the
tunnel. (through/under) 5. The class begins \_\_\_\_\_ 9 o'clock.
(at/in)

**Answers:** 1. on 2. on 3. in 4. through 5. at

## 15. Adverbs I: Manner, Place and Time

### What is an adverb?

An **adverb** gives us more information about an action. It can tell us
how, where or when something happens.

Think about a student walking into class: - She walked **quietly**.
(How?) - She waited **outside**. (Where?) - She arrived **early**.
(When?)

### 1. Adverbs of manner -- how?

They tell us how an action is done. - The baby smiled **happily**. - The
tortoise walked **slowly**. - Please write **neatly**. - The boy
answered **politely**.

Many adverbs of manner end in **-ly**, but not all do: - The car moved
**fast**. - The child sang **well**.

### 2. Adverbs of place -- where?

They tell us where an action happens. - Come **here**. - The children
are playing **outside**. - The birds flew **away**. - Please look
**upstairs**.

### 3. Adverbs of time -- when?

They tell us when an action happens. - We will leave **tomorrow**. - I
finished my work **yesterday**. - The bus arrived **early**. - Dad is
coming **soon**.

### A quick way to find them

Ask a question about the action: - How did she speak? **Softly.** -
Where did they play? **Outside.** - When did he arrive? **Yesterday.**

### Try it

Name the type of adverb in bold. 1. The girl danced **gracefully**. 2.
Please come **inside**. 3. We will visit Grandma **tomorrow**. 4. The
horse ran **fast**. 5. The children are waiting **here**.

**Answers:** 1. Manner 2. Place 3. Time 4. Manner 5. Place
