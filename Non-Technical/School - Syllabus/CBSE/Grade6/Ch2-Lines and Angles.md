
## Chapter 2: Lines and Angles

### Concept 2.1 — What is a Line, Ray, and Line Segment?
- A **line** goes on forever in both directions.  
- A **ray** starts at one point and goes on forever in one direction (like a torch beam).  
- A **line segment** has two fixed endpoints (like a ruler's edge).

```
Line:          <—————————————>
Ray:           •—————————————>
Line Segment:  •——————————————•
```

**Example 1:** The edge of your notebook page is a line segment (it has a start and an end).  
**Example 2:** Sunlight from the sun appears to travel like a ray — starting at the sun, going outward.  
**Example 3:** A railway track (if imagined endlessly) represents a line.

### Concept 2.2 — What is an Angle?
An **angle** is formed when two rays meet at a common point (called the **vertex**).

<div style="text-align:center">

```
        ray 1
          \
           \
            • ---- ray 2   (vertex is the dot)
```
*Fig 2.1 — Two rays meeting at a vertex form an angle*
</div>

**Example 1:** An open pair of scissors forms an angle at the hinge.  
**Example 2:** The hands of a clock form an angle at 3 o'clock (a right angle, 90°).  
**Example 3:** The corner of a book page is an angle (usually 90°).

### Concept 2.3 — Types of Angles
| Angle Type | Measure |
|---|---|
| Acute | Less than 90° |
| Right | Exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | More than 90° but less than 180° |
| Straight | Exactly 180° |

**Example 1:** A door opened slightly (30°) → Acute angle  
**Example 2:** A door opened fully flat against the wall (180°) → Straight angle  
**Example 3:** The corner of a square table (90°) → Right angle
