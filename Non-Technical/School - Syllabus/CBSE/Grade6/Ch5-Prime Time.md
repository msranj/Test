

## Chapter 5: Prime Time

### Concept 5.1 — Factors and Multiples
- A **factor** of a number divides it exactly, with no remainder.
- A **multiple** is what you get when you multiply a number by 1, 2, 3, and so on.

**Example 1:** Factors of 12 → 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12  
**Example 2:** Multiples of 3 → 3, 6, 9, 12, 15...  
**Example 3:** Factors of 20 → 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20

### Concept 5.2 — Prime and Composite Numbers
- A **prime number** has exactly 2 factors: 1 and itself.
- A **composite number** has more than 2 factors.
- The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.

<div style="text-align:center">

```
2   3   5   7   11   13   →  Prime numbers (only 2 factors each)
4   6   8   9   10   12  →  Composite numbers (more than 2 factors)
```

**Example 1:** Is 7 prime? Factors of 7 are just 1 and 7 → **Yes, prime**  
**Example 2:** Is 9 prime? Factors of 9 are 1, 3, 9 → **No, composite** (3 factors)  
**Example 3:** Is 11 prime? Factors are just 1 and 11 → **Yes, prime**

### Concept 5.3 — Common Factors, Common Multiples, and Co-primes
**Common factors** are factors shared by two numbers. **Common multiples** are multiples shared by two numbers. **Co-prime numbers** have only 1 as their common factor.

**Example 1:** Common factors of 12 and 18 → 1, 2, 3, 6  
**Example 2:** Common multiples of 3 and 5 → 15, 30, 45...  
**Example 3:** Are 8 and 9 co-prime? Factors of 8: 1,2,4,  8. Factors of 9: 1,3,9. Only common factor is 1 → **Yes, co-prime**

### Concept 5.4 — Prime Factorisation
Breaking a number down into a product of only prime numbers.

**Example 1:** 12 = 2 × 2 × 3  
**Example 2:** 30 = 2 × 3 × 5  
**Example 3:** 45 = 3 × 3 × 5
