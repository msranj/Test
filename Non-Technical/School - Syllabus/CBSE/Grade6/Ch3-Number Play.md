
## Chapter 3: Number Play

### Concept 3.1 — Estimating and Rounding Numbers
**Rounding** means adjusting a number to the nearest simple value (like nearest 10, 100) to make it easier to work with.

**Example 1:** 47 rounded to the nearest 10 → **50**  
**Example 2:** 123 rounded to the nearest 100 → **100**  
**Example 3:** 6,499 rounded to the nearest 1000 → **6,000**

### Concept 3.2 — Number Games and Puzzles (Magic squares, palindromes)
A **palindrome number** reads the same forwards and backwards.

**Example 1:** 121 is a palindrome (121 reversed is still 121)  
**Example 2:** 1331 is a palindrome  
**Example 3:** Take any number, add its reverse repeatedly — many numbers eventually become palindromes: 48 + 84 = 132; 132 + 231 = 363 (palindrome!)

### Concept 3.3 — Supercells and Number Comparisons
A "supercell" (as used in the textbook) is a number in a grid that's bigger than all its neighbouring numbers.

**Example 1:** In the row 5, 9, 3 — 9 is a supercell since it's bigger than both 5 and 3.  
**Example 2:** In 12, 8, 15, 4 — both 12 and 15 could be supercells depending on their other neighbours.  
**Example 3:** Comparing temperatures recorded through the week to find the "hottest" day uses the same idea — finding the biggest value among neighbours.
