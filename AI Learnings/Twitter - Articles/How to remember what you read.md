# How to Remember Everything You Read

*Summary of "How to Remember Everything You Read," Polymathinvestor.com, Nov 2025*

## The 5 Techniques

**1. Preview First (Survey the Structure)**
Spend 5–10 min skimming headings, TOC, intro/summary before reading in full. Builds a mental scaffold.
*Example:* Before reading a 40-page industry report, skim section headers and the executive summary first — you'll know it covers market size, competitors, and risks before diving in, so new facts have a "slot" to land in.

**2. Ask Questions & Set Intentions**
Turn each heading into a question, then read to answer it.
*Example:* Section titled "Supply Chain Risks" → ask "What are the top 3 risks and why?" — then read hunting for that answer instead of passively scanning.

**3. Layered Reading (Multiple Passes)**
Pass 1: quick skim for gist. Pass 2: full read, don't stop at hard parts. Pass 3: deep re-read only on the toughest/most relevant sections.
*Example:* Reading a dense legal contract — skim clause headers first, read the whole thing once without getting stuck, then re-read only the liability and termination clauses closely.

**4. Active > Passive Reading**
Annotate, paraphrase in your own words, connect ideas across sources as you go — treat it like a conversation.
*Example:* While reading a book on habits, jot in the margin: "This is like the compounding idea from that finance book I read — same principle, different domain."

**5. The 20% Highlighting Rule**
Highlight only the most essential 10–20% of text — roughly one key sentence per paragraph, not whole blocks.
*Example:* In a 500-word article section, highlight just the single sentence that states the main finding, not the surrounding explanation.

## Why These Work
Each technique forces **active processing** (framing, questioning, re-encoding, selecting) instead of passive eye-movement over text — which is what actually builds durable memory traces, per research cited in the piece (e.g., SQ3R method).

## Practical Implementation Plan
| Step | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Survey structure (headings/TOC/summary) | 5–10 min |
| 2 | Write 2–3 questions per section | 2 min |
| 3 | Skim → full read → targeted re-read | varies |
| 4 | Margin notes + paraphrase key ideas | ongoing |
| 5 | Highlight ≤20%, one line per paragraph | ongoing |

**Real-world example — reading a quarterly earnings report:**
1. Skim the headers (Revenue, Margins, Guidance, Risks) — 5 min.
2. Ask: "Did revenue beat estimates? Why did margins move?"
3. Skim once fully, then re-read the Guidance and Risks sections closely.
4. Note in margin: "Margin drop mirrors what competitor X reported last quarter."
5. Highlight only the one sentence with the actual guidance number — not the surrounding commentary.
