# 1. Non-fiction books  


### Prompt 1: Complete book knowledge extraction  
- Use this when you want to understand the entire book without missing important - topics.  
- Act as an expert educator, book analyst, and knowledge extraction specialist.
- I have uploaded a non-fiction book. Your task is to teach me the complete knowledge - contained in this book without unnecessarily skipping essential topics.
- Follow these instructions: 
    - First, analyse the entire book, including its table of contents, chapters, -  subchapters, examples, case studies, and conclusions.
    - Create a complete chapter-wise learning roadmap.
    - Identify every important concept, argument, framework, principle, model, and   methodology.
    - Explain each concept in simple language, assuming I am new to the subject.
    - Include the author's important examples, stories, evidence, and case studies.
    - Explain why each concept matters and where it can be applied.
    - Identify connections between different chapters.
    - Highlight important lessons, misconceptions, limitations, and exceptions.
    - Do not confuse a short summary with comprehensive learning.
    - Do not skip essential concepts simply to make the output shorter.

For every chapter, provide:

- Chapter overview
- Important concepts
- Detailed explanations
- Examples and real-world applications
- Key takeaways
- Important terminology
- Practical lessons
- Questions to test understanding

At the end, provide:

Complete book knowledge map  
- 20 most important lessons
- 10 ideas worth remembering for life
- A practical action plan

If the book is too large, teach it in multiple parts. Clearly identify completed and pending chapters.

Never claim that a chapter has been analysed unless you have actually processed its content.


### Prompt 2: Author's thinking and deeper understanding  
Act as a critical thinking teacher.

Analyse this book beyond its surface-level summary.

Help me understand:

- What is the central message of the book?
- What problem is the author trying to solve?
- What are the author's fundamental beliefs?
- What are the most important arguments?
- What evidence supports these arguments?
- What assumptions does the author make?
- Which ideas challenge conventional thinking?
- Which ideas are universally applicable and which depend on context?
- What are the limitations or weaknesses of the author's arguments?
- How do the ideas connect to everyday life?

Explain the author's thinking using simple language, practical examples, and relatable situations.

Finish with a section called "How to Think Like the Author."


### Prompt 3: Practical application  

Act as a practical learning and implementation coach.

Convert the knowledge from this book into actions I can implement in real life.

For every major concept:

- Explain the concept.
- Explain why it is useful.
- Give one everyday-life example.
- Give one professional or workplace example.
- Explain how I can implement it.
- Identify common mistakes.
- Suggest one exercise to practise it.
- Define how I can measure improvement.

Create:

- A 7-day implementation plan
- A 30-day implementation plan
- A habit checklist
- A self-assessment framework

Prioritise actions that provide meaningful results rather than simply collecting theoretical knowledge.

### Prompt 4: Master the book through questions
Act as a Socratic teacher.

Teach me this book through carefully designed questions.

For every chapter:

- Ask basic understanding questions.
- Ask why and how questions.
- Ask questions requiring practical application.
- Ask questions that challenge assumptions.
- Ask scenario-based questions.
- Ask questions connecting this chapter with previous chapters.
- Ask one question at a time. Wait for my answer.

Evaluate my answer, identify gaps, explain the correct reasoning, and give hints where necessary.

Do not move to the next concept until I demonstrate reasonable understanding.

At the end, assess my overall understanding of the book.
